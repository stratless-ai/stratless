# Who you are working with
_(managed by stratless, refreshed by `stratless update`. Not edited by hand.)_

## WHAT THEY KNOW

Holds the system's architecture actively in mind, restating a proposed design (the law/miner/judge structure, a Postgres call) accurately before moving on. Carries granular competitive intelligence, acquisition ranges, funding figures, star counts, specific rival timing, and uses it directly in strategic reasoning.

## HOW THEY THINK

Restates a new design in their own words, self-checks it, then pushes past confirmation into the next open question; confirmation is a checkpoint, not a rest. In strategy talk, when you offer an aspirational reframe (earned confidence, frontier alignment, invisible-infrastructure virality), they acknowledge it briefly and pivot back to concrete ground: acquisition numbers, competitive timing, adoption as proof. They've also begun stress-testing their own launch claims against the harshest objection before letting them stand.

## HOW THEY WORK

A settled recommendation usually gets a terse "go" or "continue" before they move to execution, though after a gap they sometimes ask for a context refresh first. When work stalls, they ask for a concrete status update or step-by-step plan rather than re-debating what you explained. They drive the release pipeline hands-on, asking what a staged step does before giving go-ahead, and personally check a live artifact against your status report rather than trust it. They often want a checkpoint verified before moving into the next phase.

## DIRECTION

They reshape scope mid-conversation rather than accepting a plan wholesale: adding a command, deferring an optimization, dropping a tangent to return to what matters. They redirect your technical defaults toward their own preference, yielding only when a real constraint forces it. Right now attention pulls hard toward competitive timing and acquisition economics.

## FAILURE SIGNALS

Catches gaps or overstatements in your status claims (an unpublished page, a missed release note) and stops to demand verification. Asks directly what an unfamiliar term means rather than guessing. Watch for the reboot: an earlier explanation sometimes doesn't stick, and the same question resurfaces later. A dense answer to a narrow question gets bypassed, sometimes via a pasted artifact and a blunt yes/no instead.

## TRIGGERS

A technical default without a hard constraint draws a redirect. An unverified status claim draws a challenge. An elaborate answer to a narrow question gets trimmed back or ignored in favor of a follow-up that assumes they already know the answer and just want confirmation.
